= U06: Dimensions in the nanoCAD/ESKD style
:toc:

Issue: https://github.com/Artem535/SketchCAD/issues/34[#34]. Requirements: S01-DIM, S01-UI.
Depends on S02 (xref:dimensions.adoc[]), U02 (xref:constraint-ui.adoc[]), U05 (xref:tablet-shell.adoc[]).
Decision: xref:adr-0004-dimension-style.adoc[ADR-0004].

Addresses user remark 6 from U02 testing ("drawings should look like nanoCAD").
Not in this increment: storing or dragging dimension positions, automatic de-cluttering of overlapping dimensions,
diameter dimensions, paper-space units (1.0 drawings).

== Core: `layout_dimensions`
`std::vector<DimensionGraphic> layout_dimensions(const Sketch&, const ViewTransform&, const DimensionStyle& = {})`
(`include/sketchcad/dimension_layout.h`, `src/dimension_layout.cpp`), Qt-free, screen pixels (Y down).

* One `DimensionGraphic` per dimension constraint (`kLength`, `kDistance`, `kAngle`, `kRadius`) in constraint order;
  other constraints produce nothing. Fields: `id`, `kind`, `value` (mm or radians), `segments` (pairs of points),
  `arcs` (`center`, `radius`, `start`, `sweep`, screen radians), `arrows` (filled triangles, tip first),
  `text_position` (bottom centre of the text box) and `text_angle` (screen radians in [−π/2, π/2)).
* `DimensionStyle` defaults (px): `offset` 32, `overshoot` 6, `arrow_length` 12, `arrow_half_angle` 10°, `text_gap` 4,
  `angle_radius` 0.6 × the shorter ray clamped to [32, 120].
* *Side*: for linear dimensions the dimension line goes to the side of the measured segment that faces away from the
  centre of the sketch bounds (screen); if the centre lies on the segment's line, the side whose normal points up
  (screen −Y), or left for a vertical segment.
* *Length / point–point distance* between screen points A and B with unit normal n on the chosen side:
  extension lines A → A + n·(offset + overshoot) and B → B + n·(offset + overshoot); dimension line
  A + n·offset → B + n·offset; arrows with tips at both ends of the dimension line, pointing outward along it.
  If |AB| < 2·arrow_length + 8, arrows sit outside pointing inward and the dimension line extends by arrow_length + 10
  beyond each end.
* *Point–line distance*: dimension line from the point P to its foot F on the infinite line, arrows at P and F pointing
  away from the middle (outside, as above, when short); if F lies outside the segment, an extension line from the nearer
  endpoint to F extended by overshoot. A distance of zero (point on the line) draws no dimension line, only the text.
* *Radius*: direction u = from the centre to the arc's mid-angle point (arc) or screen up-right at −45° (circle);
  dimension line from the centre C to C + u·r, one arrow with its tip on the curve pointing outward.
  If r < 2·arrow_length, the line goes from the curve outward to C + u·(r + arrow_length + 24) with the arrow tip on the
  curve pointing inward, and the text sits over the outer part.
* *Angle*: vertex V = intersection of the two infinite lines; each ray points from V to that line's farther endpoint.
  If the angle between the rays equals the dimension value (within 1e-6 rad) the arc spans the two rays, otherwise the
  first ray and the reversed second ray. Arc radius R = angle_radius; arrows at both arc ends, tangent, pointing
  away from the arc's middle; if a ray's farther endpoint is closer to V than R, an extension line along the ray from
  that endpoint to R + overshoot. Parallel lines (no vertex) fall back to text only at the midpoint between the lines'
  midpoints.
* *Text*: `text_angle` is the dimension line direction reduced to [−π/2, π/2), so a vertical dimension reads bottom to
  top (ESKD). The text is placed above the middle of the dimension line (or the arc) in its own frame: `text_position` =
  middle + `text_gap` · (the text's up direction). As ESKD requires, this does not depend on the side of the geometry,
  so the text of a dimension below or right of a part sits between the dimension line and the part.

== Controller and QML
* New layers `dimension_path` (SVG: extension and dimension lines, arcs) and `dimension_arrows_path` (closed
  triangles), drawn thin (1 px) in `muted`, arrows filled `muted`.
* `dimension_labels` entries gain `angle` (degrees, for QML rotation); `x`, `y` become the text position
  (bottom centre). The QML label is rotated about its bottom centre, drawn in `primary` 15 px medium without a box, and
  keeps a touch target of at least 48 px; tap opens the dimension editor (U02 behaviour).
* Dependent or violated dimensions (diagnosis) draw their text in `err`.

== Acceptance / test cases
Core (`dimension_layout_test`, GoogleTest, view scale 4 px/mm, no pan):

* A horizontal 50 mm line alone: dimension line 32 px above, 200 px long; extension lines from both endpoints 38 px
  long; two arrows with tips at the dimension line ends pointing outward; text 4 px above the middle, angle 0.
* Rectangle 40 × 20 mm with length dimensions on the bottom and right sides: the bottom one is below the rectangle,
  the right one right of it; the right text angle is −π/2.
* Short line (2 mm = 8 px): arrows outside pointing inward, dimension line extended.
* Circle radius: one segment from the centre to the circle at −45°, arrow tip on the circle.
* Small circle (radius 4 mm = 16 px < 2·arrow_length): leader outside the circle, arrow tip on the circle pointing inward.
* Angle of 90° between two lines sharing a corner: one arc centred at the corner with sweep π/2 and radius in
  [32, 120]; two arrows at the arc ends.
* Point–line distance with the foot outside the segment: perpendicular dimension line and one extension line.
* Every `text_angle` is within [−π/2, π/2); non-dimension constraints produce no graphics; layout is deterministic.

Qt (`sketch_controller_test`): after `apply("length")` on a line, `dimension_path` and `dimension_arrows_path` are
non-empty and the label carries `angle`; Undo clears them.
QML (`qml_test`): existing dimension scenarios keep passing (the label still opens the editor); screenshots of a
dimensioned rectangle in light and dark (`sketch_u06_light.png`, `sketch_u06_dark.png`) are saved and inspected.

Desktop only; Android not claimed.

== Implementation notes
* The controller lays dimensions out once per scene refresh (also on pan and zoom) for the two path layers, and again
  for `dimension_labels`; both use the same `layout_dimensions` call, so lines and texts cannot disagree.
* Dimension texts get a tight `canvas`-coloured mask (85 % opacity) so the grid does not run through the digits,
  similar to the text background mask in nanoCAD.
* Angle and radius texts follow the same "above the line in the text's own frame" rule; for an angle the text is
  tangent to the arc at its middle.
