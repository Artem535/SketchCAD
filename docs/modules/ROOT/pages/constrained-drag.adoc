= U03: Constrained interactive dragging
:toc:

Issue: https://github.com/Artem535/SketchCAD/issues/11[#11]. Requirements: S01-DRAG, S01-UNDO.
Depends on U01 (xref:sketch-viewport.adoc[]), S01 (xref:constraint-solver.adoc[]), S02 (xref:dimensions.adoc[]);
uses the degenerate-solution rule of issue #29.

Fixes user remark 1 from U02 testing: dragging one endpoint of a horizontal line moved both endpoints by half the
pointer movement, because the dragged point was only an initial guess and the solver spread the correction evenly.

Not in this increment: dragging lines, circles or arcs as a whole (only points are dragged, as in U01; added later as
<<curves,U03b>>),
pointer-cancel handling beyond Escape, constraint-creating snaps (U04), device latency on a tablet (P01).

== Core: `drag_point`
`SolveResult drag_point(Sketch& sketch, EntityId point, Position target)` (`include/sketchcad/drag.h`, `src/drag.cpp`).

* Moves `point` as close to `target` as the hard constraints allow and moves the rest of the geometry as little as
  practical. Hard constraints are never relaxed: the result satisfies every constraint within the S01 tolerances
  (`kLengthTolerance`, `kAngleTolerance`) or the sketch is left unchanged.
* `point` must be a point of the sketch and `target` finite, otherwise `kInvalidInput` and nothing changes.
* A point not referenced by any constraint (directly, through a line or as a curve center) moves exactly to `target`
  without running the solver.
* Otherwise two phases:
  . *Soft phase* (bounded, at most 50 Levenberg–Marquardt iterations): hard residuals weighted 10, the drag residual
    `point − target` [mm] weighted 1, and stabilization residuals `q − q₀` [mm] for every other solver variable
    (point coordinates, radii) weighted 1e-3. These soft residuals exist only inside `drag_point`;
    they are never part of `solve`, `diagnose` or the DOF count (ADR-0003).
  . *Projection*: the soft result is solved with the normal `solve` (hard residuals only, same tolerance checks,
    same degenerate rule).
* The result is accepted only if the projection returns `kSolved` and no line or radius that was longer than
  `kLengthTolerance` in the input became no longer than it (`kDegenerate`). Any other status is returned and the sketch
  is unchanged.
* Intent, not exact contract: the dragged point follows the target along its free DOF; geometry tied to it by
  constraints follows rigidly where the constraints require; untied geometry keeps its place. A fixed point stays
  where it is (`kSolved`, unchanged), a point of a fixed-length line with a fixed other end moves on the circle
  nearest to the target.

== Gesture lifecycle (`ToolSession`, `Document`)
* begin: a press on a point with the select tool arms the drag; the first `drag` opens a `Document` gesture.
* update: every `drag` is one `gesture_step` running `drag_point` from the last accepted state (warm start) towards
  the snapped pointer position. A rejected step leaves the last valid geometry in place; the gesture continues and the
  next pointer position is tried again from that state. A rejected intermediate state is never shown or committed.
* commit: `release` ends the gesture; the whole drag is one undo step (none if nothing moved).
* cancel: Escape during a drag cancels the gesture and restores the geometry from before the press.
* With the app's commit step (the solver) installed, an accepted step is re-checked by it; it is already solved, so
  the check does not move geometry.

== Acceptance / test cases
Core (`drag_test`, GoogleTest, with an independent residual oracle in the test):

* Free point (no constraints): moves exactly to the target; other geometry untouched.
* Horizontal line: dragging one endpoint vertically by 6 mm moves both endpoints by 6 mm (± 1e-3 mm) and the other
  endpoint keeps its x; dragging horizontally moves only the dragged endpoint.
* Length dimension kept: dragging an endpoint of a 40 mm line changes its direction or position, the length stays
  40 mm within `kLengthTolerance`.
* Fixed point: dragging a point with `kFix` leaves the whole sketch unchanged (`kSolved`).
* Fixed pivot: a 30 mm line with a fixed end; dragging the free end towards a far target puts it on the 30 mm circle,
  on the ray from the pivot to the target (± 1e-3 mm).
* Rectangle (horizontal/vertical sides, coincident corners, one fixed corner): dragging the opposite corner resizes
  it; all constraints hold; the fixed corner does not move.
* Singular / invalid: unknown or non-point ID, or non-finite target → `kInvalidInput`, unchanged.
  Dragging an endpoint of a horizontal line exactly onto its other endpoint would collapse the line: the step is
  rejected (`kInvalidInput` or `kDegenerate`) and the sketch is unchanged.
* Unconstrained geometry elsewhere in the sketch is never touched.
* Cost: a sketch of 20 constrained rectangles, one corner dragged in 20 steps; the mean step time is recorded as a test
  property; no pass/fail threshold.

`sketch_tool_test`: with `solver_step()` installed, a drag of a horizontal line's endpoint is one undo step and Undo
restores both endpoints; Escape-cancel (`cancel()`) restores the start geometry and adds no history; a step onto the other
endpoint (collapse) keeps the last valid geometry and a later valid step still moves the point.

Qt (`sketch_controller_test`): dragging an endpoint of a horizontal line through the controller moves both endpoints
vertically by the pointer movement in mm.
QML (`qml_test`, real pointer moves offscreen): draw a line, apply `horizontal`, drag an endpoint with the mouse; the
other endpoint follows vertically and the line stays horizontal.

All existing CTest checks stay green. Desktop only; touch input and latency on a device are not claimed (P01).

== Implementation notes
* The soft phase lives in the internal `detail::Problem` (`run_drag`): hard terms get a `ceres::ScaledLoss`, anchors are
  plain auto-diff residuals; `solve`, `diagnose` and `run` never see them.
* The hard weight in the soft phase is deliberately moderate (10): with 1e3 the penalty made Levenberg–Marquardt creep
  along curved constraint manifolds and a large single step (pivot fixture, 90° turn) stopped far from the target within
  the 50-iteration bound. Accuracy comes from the projection, not from the penalty weight.
* No new `DragSession` class and no `DragHandler.qml` (backlog suggestion): the lifecycle already exists as
  `ToolSession::press/drag/release/cancel` over a `Document` gesture, and `SketchViewport.qml`'s `PointHandler` already
  delivers the pointer; only the gesture step changed.

Baseline: 106/106 CTest checks on the stack below (#28 → #30 → #31).

Cost: 20 constrained rectangles (160 point parameters), one corner dragged in 20 steps: about 10 ms per step on the

solving only the connected component of the dragged point is a possible later optimization. No target is claimed.
Not claimed: touch drag and latency on a tablet (P01), pointer-cancel from the platform (only Escape is tested).

== Dragging curves as a whole (U03b)
Issue: https://github.com/Artem535/SketchCAD/issues/46[#46]. User remark after U04 part 1 testing: dragging a circle by its outline did nothing (only points could be
dragged), and grabbing it near a point lying on it dragged that point instead, which rebuilt the attached geometry
around it.

* Core: `SolveResult drag_points(Sketch&, const std::map<EntityId, Position>& targets)` generalizes `drag_point` to
  several dragged points (each drag residual weighted 1, everything else as above, same projection and acceptance).
  `drag_point(s, p, t)` is `drag_points(s, {{p, t}})`. An empty map, a non-point ID or a non-finite target →
  `kInvalidInput`, unchanged. Points not referenced by any constraint move exactly to their targets.
* `ToolSession`: with the select tool, a press on a line, circle or arc (picked as today: a point within the pick
  tolerance still wins and is dragged as a point) arms a curve drag. The pointer displacement d from the press moves
  a line's two endpoints, or a circle's / arc's centre, to their start positions + d, as one gesture
  "Move curve" with the U03 lifecycle (warm start, rejected steps keep the last valid geometry, Escape cancels, one undo
  step). Radii are not targeted; constraints decide what else follows: a point kept on the circle by `kOnCurve` moves
  with it, and a line ending there stretches (U04). No snapping during a curve drag: the displacement is used raw.
* A press without movement only selects, as before; the selection stays on the curve after a drag.

=== Acceptance / test cases (U03b)
Core (`drag_test`): `drag_points` translating both endpoints of a 10 mm line with a length dimension keeps the
length and moves both by the displacement (± 1e-3 mm); invalid input as above; `drag_point` behaviour is unchanged
(existing cases).
`sketch_tool_test`: dragging a circle's outline moves its centre by the pointer displacement, keeps the radius, is one
"Move curve" undo step, and a point on it (`kOnCurve`) follows; dragging a line translates it; Escape restores; a tap
on the outline adds no history.
Qt (`sketch_controller_test`): a press-drag-release on a circle's outline through the controller moves its centre.
QML (`qml_test`): draw a circle, drag its outline with the mouse; the centre moves by the pointer displacement.
Desktop only.
